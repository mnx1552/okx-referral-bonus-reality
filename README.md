## Three ways exchanges pay a referral, and why the biggest number is often not cash

Crypto exchanges settle referrals in one of three shapes, and knowing which one you are looking at explains most of the confusion in comparison tables.

**Welcome tasks.** You register, verify, deposit and trade, and each step unlocks a slice of the advertised total. The giant numbers live here. Bitget advertises a 6,200 USDT pack, Toobit up to 15,000 USDT in vouchers and Bitunix up to 10,000 USDT. In each case most of that value is a futures bonus: margin that can only be used to open positions, cannot be withdrawn, and usually leaves you keeping only the profit you make with it. OKX's $5-to-$400 ladder is the same shape, paid in BTC rather than trading credit.

**Fee kickback.** Your inviter earns commission on your trading fees and hands part of it back. KuCoin lets the referrer share up to their full 25% to 35% rate for 365 days. HTX runs a 30% rebate pool for 730 days, and XT splits 20% of fees between the two of you. At OKX, affiliates can attach a fee rebate to their code, and the percentage is set by the affiliate. A code with 0% attached pays you nothing beyond the new-user reward, so the code you sign up with changes what the same account is worth. OKX's separate onchain referral program for its Web3 wallet and DEX states commission of up to 50% of invitees' trading fees for the inviter, with the invitee's fee discount set by whoever runs the code and paid straight into the wallet.

**One-time crypto reward.** Regulated exchanges tend to pay a modest, clean amount in real crypto. Gemini pays $50 after $100 of trades. Kraken's main app offer pays up to $20 USDG. Binance.US pays $10 to each side. These are smaller headline numbers and far more likely to be withdrawable.

The payout model matters more than the number. Take Anna, who buys $300 of bitcoin four times a year. At OKX's Regular taker rate of 0.10%, her annual fees come to about $1.20. Even a 20% kickback returns 24 cents a year, so a flat $5 in BTC is worth many times more to her than any rebate. Ben trades $40,000 of spot a month. At 0.10% that is $40 in monthly fees, $480 a year, and a 20% kickback returns $96 annually for as long as he keeps trading. Match the payout model to your own volume first, then compare the sizes.

## Where people get caught out

**Most sign-up bonuses are not instantly withdrawable.** Finder's own guidance is blunt: very few free crypto sign-up bonuses allow instant withdrawals, and the fine print may include a minimum period your account must stay open plus specific eligibility and redemption windows. Budget for the reward to be locked or restricted until you have traded.

**Futures bonuses are not cash.** The Bitget, Toobit and Bitunix numbers are mostly margin that cannot be withdrawn; you usually keep only the profit made with it. A 6,200 USDT headline can be worth less than a $50 payment in bitcoin.

**A/B tests mean your friend may see a different offer.** Crypto.com's App bonus appears as up to 1 BTC in CRO for one test group and up to $180 in mystery boxes for another, with different qualifying conditions for each. A third tracker lists yet another version at $75 in CRO plus $25 for staking. Screenshots are not terms.

**A referral code may be addable after sign-up, or may not.** Crypto.com gives referees 10 days to attach a referral ID after creating an account. Nothing in the material reviewed here shows an equivalent grace period at OKX, so use the link before you register rather than assuming it can be fixed later.

**App and Exchange are separate programs at the same brand.** Crypto.com's App pays CRO on a volume ladder; its Exchange pays 50% of net fees for 12 months plus up to $2,000 in CRO. A code for one does not carry to the other.
